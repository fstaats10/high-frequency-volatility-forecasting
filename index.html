High-Frequency Volatility Forecasting with an Option-Data-Augmented Neural Network

A quantitative study bridging traditional Econometrics and Deep Learning. Investigating the prediction of 5-minute realized volatility on the S&P 500 by comparing traditional GARCH models with a Stacked GRU Recurrent Neural Network enriched with high-frequency option data.

The Problem & Relevance

Why 5-minute volatility?
Accurate volatility forecasts are vital for derivative pricing, portfolio allocation, and financial risk management.

The Challenge:
Traditional volatility models (like GARCH) rely primarily on historical returns. In contrast, option prices embed forward-looking information about market expectations of future uncertainty. However, extracting reliable signals from high-frequency option data is highly complex due to market microstructure noise and chaotic intraday patterns.

Tech Stack & Methodology

Python TensorFlow / Keras Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) GARCH / GARCH-X Financial Time Series Bayesian Optimization Diebold-Mariano testing

Insights & Robustness

Showcasing critical evaluation and model resilience under varying market regimes.

Statistical Significance

The Diebold-Mariano test confirms a statistically significant improvement of the Augmented NN over GARCH (p < 0.001) . Adding option data gives a modest but significant gain .

GARCH Stability

While the NN performs best overall, robustness testing reveals GARCH is more stable across alternative samples and high/low volatility regimes .

Tail-Event Sensitivity

Neural Networks are more sensitive to extreme tail events. Tests applied include Tariff spike filtering, Winsorization, and High/Low volatility regimes . Also, GARCH-X showed risk of in-sample overfitting .
